# Chassis Flow Geometry: Harmonizing Graphics with Car Anatomy

A professional car livery designer never treats a vehicle as a flat canvas. A car is an aerodynamic sculpture sculpted from sheet steel, aluminum, and carbon fiber, shaped by airflow, downforce, and structural hardpoints.

---

## 1. The Primary Anatomical Hardpoints

```
               [Greenhouse / Glass Canopy]
                     /------------\
                    /   Roofline   \
 [Front Fender]    /                \    [C-Pillar / Haunch]
     ______       /   Beltline        \       ______
    /      \     /=====================\     /      \
===/  ( )   \===/       Mid-Door        \===/   ( )  \===
  Front Wheel     [Swage / Character Line]   Rear Wheel
  [Splitter]           [Rocker Panel]        [Diffuser]
```

### 1. The Beltline (Glass-to-Metal Boundary)
- **Anatomy:** The horizontal ledge where the side windows meet the door metal.
- **Livery Rule:** The beltline acts as the natural division for **Two-Tone colorways** (e.g., black roof/greenhouse over colorful lower body, as seen in modern GT3 racers and Kaido racers). Running a graphic across the beltline into the glass is only permitted via perforated window vinyl (One-Way Vision film) or window netting.

### 2. The Swage / Character Line
- **Anatomy:** The distinct longitudinal body crease running along the flank from headlight to taillight, designed by automotive stylists to catch the sun and give the car stance.
- **Livery Rule:**
  - **Option A (Alignment):** Pin-stripes and color separation boundaries should trace exactly along the swage line.
  - **Option B (Dynamic Slash):** If breaking the swage line, the graphic cut MUST strike across it at an angle between **25° and 45°**. A lazy or shallow 5° cut looks like an accidental misprint or a misaligned body panel.

### 3. The Wheel Arch Crowns & Blisters
- **Anatomy:** The semicircular flares over the front and rear wheels (standard fenders, factory widebody blisters, or bolted-on overfenders like Pandem / Liberty Walk).
- **Livery Rule:**
  - **Concentric Energy:** Circular decals (meatballs, sanctioning roundels) should either sit comfortably inside the door panel away from the arch, or graphic stripes must arch concentrically around the flare.
  - **Fender Flares:** On widebody kits, the flare itself can be treated with contrasting accent paint or raw carbon fiber, grounding the car's aggressive stance.

### 4. The Rocker Panel & Side Skirt
- **Anatomy:** The lowest horizontal beam along the bottom edge of the doors between the wheels.
- **Livery Rule:**
  - Darker colors (matte black, carbon, dark grey) on the rocker panel visually lower the vehicle, pulling its center of gravity toward the ground.
  - The rocker panel is the prime location for secondary technical sponsor lines (e.g. `BILSTEIN  BBS  BREMBO  MOTUL`).

### 5. The C-Pillar & Rear Haunch (The Power Center)
- **Anatomy:** The diagonal structural pillar connecting the roof to the rear deck, arching over the rear driving wheels.
- **Livery Rule:**
  - This is the focal point of rear-wheel-drive visual tension.
  - In JGTC and DTM liveries, the dynamic wedge graphics peak in volume and saturation at the rear haunch, visually communicating that horsepower is delivered through the rear contact patch.

### 6. The Hood / Bonnet Billboard
- **Anatomy:** The wide, flat frontal expanse between headlights and windshield.
- **Livery Rule:**
  - The hood is the primary high-speed billboard visible in rear-view mirrors and TV head-on cameras.
  - **Orientation:** In circuit racing, the title sponsor on the hood is oriented to read from the front (facing a spectator looking at the nose). In street/drift culture, it is sometimes reversed or placed diagonally along the hood vent vents.
  - Keep decals clear of central louvers and functional NACA ducts.

## 2. Aerodynamic Appendages (Aero Integration)

Modern race cars and tuned street machines feature pronounced aerodynamic surfaces:
- **Front Splitter & Canards:**
  - Must be finished in high-contrast accent colors (fluorescent yellow, neon red) or satin carbon to highlight downforce blades.
  - Red tow hook arrows point directly to the tow eye mounting point on the splitter flank.
- **Rear GT Wing:**
  - **Top Blade Surface:** Large, bold sponsor name (e.g. `CALSONIC`, `CASTROL`, `ADVAN`, `HONDA`).
  - **Endplates:** Highly visible vertical surfaces for manufacturer skunkworks emblems (`NISMO`, `MUGEN`, `TRD`, `STI`) or tire partners.
  - **Underside of Blade:** Often painted in an accent pop color (e.g. bright red or fluorescent orange) visible only when the car pitches forward under heavy braking!
